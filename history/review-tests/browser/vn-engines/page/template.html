Box Beside the Genre

Two open-source visual-novel engines, WebGAL 4.6.7 (MPL-2.0) and Monogatari 2.8.1 (MIT), run here in the same emulated phone as your extension, with SillyTavern's own background, Seraphina's sprites and the lines from your test chat. Each card puts their real default screens beside yours (pink frame). Tap a screen to enlarge it. Your sourced values (box type for 58 cm, AUTO at 260 words a minute) stay as they are.

Run side by side

Phone held upright

Different on purpose

Both engines are made for a landscape 16:9 screen: Monogatari's own default setting is Orientation: 'landscape', and WebGAL rotates its stage when the phone stands upright (*measured* here at 412 × 915). Yours: laid out for the upright phone from the start, inside SillyTavern's chat column.

A long passage (NVL)

Different on purpose

The engines got a 342-character sentence (long enough to turn your box tall: 340 or more); your screen shows the 444-character test sentence. WebGAL draws its sentence on 5 rows in a box with room for 2; after 30 s, 62 of its 132 word pieces sit inside the box (*measured*), so the author has to split long lines. Monogatari switches to NVL only when the script says nvl. Yours: a sentence of 340 characters or more turns the box tall by itself, chosen by length.

Sources: DEV, ADV vs NVL · Fuwanovel, NVL vs ADV

Hide the box

Not in yours

Both engines put Hide in their default menu (*measured*: each screen above came from pressing it). Yours: the box stays; the scene and the character's picture behind it cannot be seen whole.

Source: itch.io, hiding the box on touch

Box opacity

Not in yours
The glade is quiet in the late light.

Light sky blue on your panel:

WebGAL lets the player set the box's opacity. Monogatari's default box is black at 50 %; white text on it over a white scene gives 4.0:1, under the 4.5:1 that WCAG asks for (*derived*). Yours: fixed at 74 %, the least at which light sky blue keeps 4.5:1 over white; drag the slider to see where it breaks.

Sources: WCAG 2.2, 1.4.3 · itch.io, textbox opacity

Text speed and AUTO settings

Not in yours

Both engines give two sliders, one for how fast letters appear and one for how long AUTO waits. Yours: said words appear at the speaker's own syllable rate, other text at silent-reading pace, and AUTO waits reading time at 260 words a minute (Brysbaert 2019), about 42 ms a letter (*derived*); no slider changes these.

Sources: itch.io, auto-advance timing · Brysbaert 2019, J Mem Lang 109:104047

Backlog

Different on purpose

WebGAL's ↺ returns the story to the chosen line (*measured*: pressed here, the box went back to Seraphina's first line), and its menu bar has Replay for the current line's voice. Yours: Log shows the whole chat as it sounds, and ◀ steps back through the boxes; no replay of a spoken line.

Sources: Giant Bomb, Backlog · itch.io, polishing your release

Text size and font choice

Not in yours

Ren'Py also offers a dyslexia font, high contrast and self-voicing (its docs). Yours: SillyTavern's own font scale and the system's forced colours apply; axe-core finds no violation; there is no size, font or contrast switch of its own.

Sources: Accessible design in visual novels · Tips on making your VN more accessible

Continue mark

Yours goes further

Neither engine shows a mark in these default screens, each taken after its line had finished (*measured*). Yours: a blinking ▼, still for readers who ask for less motion.

Published games (screenshots from Wikipedia)

Text box and name

Same

Four of the five put the name in its own tab or line above the text, at the left; Clannad names the speaker inside the line, the Japanese way. Narration goes unnamed (Katawa Shoujo). Yours: the same: a slanted tag at the left for said words, no name for narration.

Date on screen

Same

All three keep the story's date in the top-left corner; Persona 5 adds the time of day ("Afternoon"). Yours: the clock chip in the same corner gives the hour and adds the place, read from the engine's clock; the Story clock setting hides it.

Glossary (TIPS)

Yours goes further

Steins;Gate's TIPS list the story's terms, written ahead and unlocked as they appear. Yours: select a word for its path (what the engine decided and why), hold a marked word for its marks; Memory lists what the player's character knows with its source.

Sources: In-game encyclopedia anatomy · Steins;Gate TIPS

Flowchart and timeline

Different on purpose

Zero Escape's flowchart jumps to any finished section; critics note it gives away what lies ahead. WebGAL has a Flowchart screen too; it needs chapters marked in the script, so it was not set up here. Yours: one line of what happened, dragged or stepped; nothing ahead is shown.

Sources: Game Developer, Zero Time Dilemma · Fuwanovel, built-in flowcharts

Meeting a character

Same

Danganronpa and Persona introduce a character full-screen: portrait, name, role. Yours: the same family: picture with its shadow, tilted white label, trade above it; once a chat.

Sources: GamesRadar, Atlus UI · Hardcore Gaming 101, Danganronpa

Highlighted words

Different on purpose

Ace Attorney colours the testimony a player can challenge; Steins;Gate colours the words in a mail that the player can answer; Danganronpa colours the statements to attack, and critics say the colour points at the answer. Yours: gold marks words the engine decided, with nothing to answer or attack.

Sources: Kotaku, Danganronpa · Giant Bomb review

Scene change card

Same

Many games show a title card for a new place or a jump in time; the two engines above only fade the scene. Yours: "Arriving.." for a new place, "Passing.." for an hour or more, with the date and light.

Dialogue as a column (desktop)

Different on purpose

Disco Elysium sets its dialogue in a tall column at the right of a 16:9 screen. Yours: the box at the foot on every screen; a phone has no room for a side column.

Sources: 80.lv, Disco Elysium UI · Disco Elysium's text box
